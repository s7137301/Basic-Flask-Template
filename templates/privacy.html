Privacy Policy

This Privacy Policy explains how Potato collects, uses, protects, shares, and retains information when you use our peer-to-peer compute marketplace, APIs, dashboards, and support channels.

1. Information Potato collects

1.1 Account data. We collect names, email addresses, authentication records, profile photos, company names, enterprise review information, permission levels, account status, and seller ratings.

1.2 Hardware and listing data. We collect CPU and GPU descriptions, RAM, benchmark scores, hourly prices, availability, queue settings, failover settings, listing status, and seller identifiers.

1.3 Telemetry and network data. To operate the service, Potato may process node health, uptime, workload state, performance measurements, connection metadata, IP addresses, user-agent details, timestamps, approximate location derived from network information, and security logs. Telemetry is used for reliability, support, abuse detection, SLA evaluation, and capacity planning.

1.4 Transaction data. We retain Agreement identifiers, hours, seller payouts, buyer fees, priority premiums, escrow state, refunds, penalties, completed hours, failover assignments, and dispute records. Payment providers may separately process card or bank details; Potato does not need to store full payment card numbers.

2. How we use information

2.1 Potato uses information to authenticate accounts, present listings, calculate fees, create rental Agreements, maintain queues, operate escrow records, administer failover, provide dashboards, investigate abuse, process ratings, respond to support requests, and comply with law.

2.2 We may use aggregated or de-identified information to understand capacity, improve scheduling, measure reliability, and develop safer marketplace controls.

3. Docker and workload privacy boundaries

3.1 Where Potato or a participating Seller provides containerized execution, workloads are intended to run inside an isolated Docker container or equivalent sandbox boundary. The boundary limits filesystem, process, network, and privilege access according to the configured runtime.

3.2 Isolation is a technical control, not an absolute guarantee. Buyers must not submit secrets or highly sensitive data unless they have assessed the environment and contractual suitability. Sellers must not inspect, copy, or retain Buyer workload content except as necessary for authorised operations and incident response.

3.3 Potato may collect logs and metadata needed to troubleshoot or enforce acceptable-use rules. We do not promise that workload output is confidential from every infrastructure operator unless a separate enterprise agreement says so.

4. Data sharing

4.1 We share necessary listing and Agreement information with the participating Buyer and Seller, including names, hardware details, hours, pricing, queue state, escrow state, and failover records.

4.2 We may share information with payment processors, hosting and infrastructure providers, security vendors, professional advisers, auditors, and service providers acting under instructions and confidentiality obligations.

4.3 We may disclose information when required by law, legal process, a valid government request, an emergency involving safety, or an investigation into fraud, abuse, unauthorised access, or prohibited workloads. We do not sell personal information as a business model.

5. GDPR rights

5.1 Where the GDPR applies, Potato processes personal data on lawful bases including contract performance, legitimate interests in security and service operation, consent where requested, and legal obligations.

5.2 Subject to applicable exceptions, individuals may request access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability, or objection to processing. Consent-based processing may be withdrawn. Requests should include enough information for us to verify identity.

5.3 International transfers may occur through service providers. Potato uses contractual, technical, and organisational safeguards appropriate to the transfer and risk.

6. CCPA and regional privacy rights

6.1 Where the CCPA or similar law applies, California residents may have rights to know, access, correct, delete, and limit certain uses of sensitive personal information, subject to statutory exceptions.

6.2 Potato does not sell personal information for monetary consideration. We may use service providers for business purposes such as hosting, security, support, analytics, and payment administration.

7. Security

7.1 Potato uses access controls, authentication safeguards, logging, least-privilege practices, and operational review to protect information. No internet service or distributed hardware network can guarantee absolute security.

7.2 Users must use strong unique passwords, protect tokens, enable available account protections, and report suspected compromise promptly. Do not upload passwords, API keys, private keys, or secrets in profiles, listings, support forms, or workloads.

8. Financial escrow retention

8.1 Potato retains financial Agreement records, including totals, fees, payout calculations, escrow states, refunds, disputes, chargebacks, and penalty adjustments, for as long as reasonably necessary to operate the marketplace, reconcile accounts, prevent fraud, resolve disputes, satisfy tax and accounting obligations, and comply with law.

8.2 Retention periods may differ from ordinary account data because financial and legal records often require longer preservation. When records are no longer required, Potato will delete, anonymise, or securely dispose of them according to its operational retention practices.

9. Cookies, retention, and contact

9.1 Potato may use essential session storage and similar technologies to keep accounts secure and maintain requested functionality. Optional analytics or marketing technologies, if introduced, will be described through appropriate controls.

9.2 To exercise a privacy right or ask a question, contact Potato through the support channel and include the account email, request type, and relevant Agreement identifier. We may request verification before disclosing or changing personal information.
